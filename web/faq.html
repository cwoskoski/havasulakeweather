Common questions about Havasu Lake, California (also called Havasu Landing), the lake, and this weather station. Live readings are on the conditions page.

Is Havasu Lake, CA the same as Lake Havasu City, AZ?

No. Havasu Lake is a small community in San Bernardino County, California (ZIP 92363), on the Chemehuevi Indian Reservation along the California shore of Lake Havasu. Lake Havasu City is the much larger Arizona city, home of the London Bridge, on the opposite shore. They face each other across the lake, but driving between them means a long trip around it. This site reports weather from Havasu Lake, CA.

Is Havasu Lake the same as Havasu Landing?

Yes — locals often call the whole community Havasu Landing, after the Chemehuevi Tribe’s Havasu Landing Resort & Casino and marina on the shore. Havasu Lake, CA, Havasu Landing and Havasu Landing, CA all refer to the same place on the California side of Lake Havasu, and this site’s weather covers it.

Is the weather the same on both sides of the lake?

Usually close, but not identical. Most phone weather apps use the Lake Havasu City airport or a computer model, both on the Arizona side or averaged over a wide area. A station right on the California shore catches local wind, shoreline temperatures and storms that miss the other side.

What time zone is Havasu Lake, CA in?

Pacific Time. California observes daylight saving time and Arizona does not (it stays on Mountain Standard Time). So from March to early November the clocks on both sides of the lake match, and from November to March Arizona is one hour ahead of Havasu Lake, CA. Times on this site are always Havasu Lake local time.

Where does this weather data come from?

Current conditions come from a personal Ambient Weather WS-2902D station in Havasu Lake, CA, with a shaded WH31E sensor for air temperature and an on-site lightning detector. Readings arrive about once a minute. The forecast and weather alerts come from the National Weather Service, radar from NOAA, and lake and river data from the USGS and the Bureau of Reclamation.

Is this an official National Weather Service station?

No. It is a community station, offered as a reference for neighbors and visitors. For official warnings, follow the National Weather Service; their active alerts for the area are shown at the top of the live conditions page.

What is the Lake Havasu water level today?

See the Lake Havasu water level page for the live reading. Lake Havasu is kept close to full by Parker Dam: over the last ten years it has stayed between about 449.6 and 452.6 feet (NAVD88), a foot or so lower in midwinter than in early summer.

What does a Davis Dam “unit” mean?

River boaters describe Davis Dam releases in units — one of the dam’s generators, about 4,600 cubic feet per second each. Under 2 units the Colorado River below Laughlin runs low, with sandbars and shallows; 3 or more units is good water. The water page shows today’s daily average in units.

How warm is the water in Lake Havasu?

Lake Havasu water temperature (measured by the Bureau of Reclamation near Parker Dam) runs about 53–54°F in January and February and peaks around 84–85°F in July and August, sometimes reaching 87°F or more. The live reading is on the water page.

When is it windy on Lake Havasu?

Mornings are often calmest, with wind tending to build in the afternoon. The roughest days usually come with passing weather systems — strong, gusty north winds from late fall through spring — and with summer monsoon thunderstorm outflows. The wind page shows live gusts and when it has been windy here.

Does Havasu Lake get much rain or lightning?

Only a few inches of rain a year, mostly from winter storms and from summer monsoon thunderstorms (about July through September), which can bring lightning, sudden wind and blowing dust. The rain & storms page shows on-site lightning detection within about 25 miles, and the radar shows where storms are.
